# Extension sketch: innovation policy and the task frontier

The paper's endogenous technology choice compares the private returns to automation and new-task creation,

\[
\kappa_Iv_I(n) \quad\text{and}\quad \kappa_Nv_N(n).
\]

A tractable extension is to add policy wedges $\tau_I$ and $\tau_N$ to these returns. The interior BGP condition becomes

\[
(1+\tau_I)\kappa_Iv_I(n)=(1+\tau_N)\kappa_Nv_N(n).
\]

The first question is local: how does the BGP task gap $n$ respond to a subsidy for new-task creation? The second is welfare based: can a policy offset the labor-market externality without eliminating the productivity gains from automation?

This extension is deliberately narrower than rebuilding the model with worker retraining or sectoral heterogeneity. It changes one equilibrium condition and preserves the paper's task technology, innovation values, and stability mechanism. A complete treatment would have to compare private and social innovation values and cannot assume that the new-task subsidy is welfare improving.
